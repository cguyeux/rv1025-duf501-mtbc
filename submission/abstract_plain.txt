Whole protein families still have no known function, and where no structural or profile relative exists, nothing is left to transfer annotation from except the record of selection inside the family itself. We ask how far that record can be read in DUF501 (a domain of unknown function; Pfam PF04417), a bacterial family of 4,370 proteins with no Gene Ontology term, solved structure or fold relative, yet essential in three actinobacterial genera. In its tractable member Rv1025 of Mycobacterium tuberculosis, Cys113, His115 and Glu59 are occupied in all 1,472 near-complete bacterial sequences of the Pfam alignment, with no non-conservative substitution among the 2,228 examined: the site is a feature of the whole bacterial family. On the predicted structure the three side chains converge on one point at coordination distance; holo AlphaFold3 places a divalent metal there, a backbone-geometry predictor recovers the same site, and mutating the residues relocates it. Three further signals frame it: the gene block carrying DUF501 stays syntenic phylum-wide, in the upper fifth of random gene blocks, a second cavity is conserved independently of it, and coevolution recovers only the fold, being blind to invariant positions. Three eukaryotic members lost the cysteine-histidine pair and kept the glutamate, in one or two events on a family tree. We propose that DUF501 is a metal-binding protein and candidate metalloenzyme, the first functional hypothesis for this family; being computational, this supports rather than proves metal occupancy. The same conserved, essential pocket is a candidate antitubercular target.
